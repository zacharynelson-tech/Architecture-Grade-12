Cycle 3 - Accessible Design

Designing the route from the parking lot to the restroom stall so that everyone can use the building.

12.2.112.2.312.2.512.3.112.3.212.9

Learning Objectives

Understand

Discuss how the 2010 ADA Standards (including scoping) affect design and the routes architects design within and to a building.

Apply

Apply ADA requirements for doors, ramps, parking, stairs, handrails, and plumbing facilities to a layout.

Create

Create a fully dimensioned accessible restroom layout, modeled in Revit with a custom family, and placed on a plotted sheet.

Lesson Outline

▸
  • ADA Regulations & Scoping
    The 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design: what they require, how scoping works, and common requirements.
  • Accessible Routes
    Walking surfaces, doors, ramps, elevators, parking lots, stairs, and handrails. How the standards shape the path from the car to the building.
  • Restroom Layout Basics
    Clear floor space, turning space, reach ranges, and toilet compartments.
  • Grab Bars, Urinals & Lavatories
    Grab bar locations, urinal and lavatory requirements, and accessories. Using the ADA requirements to lay out a restroom.
  • Bathing Rooms
    Tubs and showers: the requirements for accessible bathing rooms.
  • Revit Studio Day
    Model your restroom. Build a custom family (e.g., an accessory or fixture).
  • Revit Studio Day (cont.)
    Plotting: set up a sheet with a title block and place your plan and views.
  • Wrap-Up
    Check your layout against the ADA clearances. Review for the cycle.

This is a rough outline and is subject to change.

Independent Assignments

Reading + Guided Notes (A)

Accessible Routes
▸

Read the ADA sections covering where accessible routes are required, walking surfaces, doors, ramps, elevators, parking, stairs, and handrails. Complete the guided notes on how each affects a route from the car to the building.

Source/materials: 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (uploaded PDF): 206.2 Where Required (PDF p. 59); 208 Parking Spaces scoping (p. 69); 302-305 floor surfaces, turning space, clear floor space (pp. 108-111); 403 Walking Surfaces (p. 121); 404 Doors (p. 123); 405 Ramps (p. 131); 407 Elevators (p. 137); 502 Parking Spaces (p. 153); 504 Stairways (p. 157); 505 Handrails (p. 158). Read the sections your teacher assigns, not the whole chapter.

LevelDescription
BeginningNotes largely incomplete or inaccurate; requirements confused between elements.
DevelopingMostly complete; some requirements missing or copied.
ProficientComplete and accurate; correctly states key requirements for each route element in your own words.
ExemplaryAll Proficient criteria met, plus applies the requirements to a real route you have walked.

Reading + Guided Notes (B)

Accessible Restrooms
▸

Read the restroom planning guide's sections on reach ranges, clear floor space, turning spaces, lavatories, toilet compartments, grab bars, and sample layouts. Complete the guided notes, noting where the 2017 ICC numbers differ from the 2010 ADA numbers.

Source/materials: Bobrick Planning Guide for Accessible Restrooms: Reach Ranges and Space Requirements (pp. 9-13); Planning an Accessible Restroom (pp. 13-23); Large and Small Public Restrooms (pp. 23-27); Accessible Bathing Facilities (pp. 28-42). Also 2010 ADA Standards: 213 Toilet Facilities and Bathing Facilities (PDF p. 73); 603-604 (pp. 164-165); 606 Lavatories (p. 174); 609 Grab Bars (p. 185).

LevelDescription
BeginningNotes largely incomplete; clearances and dimensions missing or wrong.
DevelopingMostly complete; some dimensions wrong or ADA and ICC values mixed up.
ProficientComplete and accurate; correct clearances for compartments, grab bars, and turning space, ADA and ICC distinguished.
ExemplaryAll Proficient criteria met, plus explains why a Bobrick sample layout does or does not meet the ADA minimums.

Software Exercise - Accessible Restroom, Custom Family & Sheet

Model, family, and plot
▸

Model an accessible public restroom in Revit that meets ADA clearances. Create at least one custom family (such as a grab bar or accessory), then place your plan on a sheet with a title block.

Source/materials: 2010 ADA Standards; Bobrick Planning Guide (see Reading B)

LevelDescription
BeginningLayout does not meet clearances, or no custom family or sheet.
DevelopingLayout mostly meets clearances with errors; family or sheet incomplete.
ProficientLayout meets ADA clearances; custom family works; sheet has a title block and correctly placed views.
ExemplaryAll Proficient criteria met, plus a family with adjustable parameters and a clean, well-organized sheet.

Scale & Proportion

Human-scale study of clearances
▸

Draw to scale a plan of a 60-inch turning space, a 30x48-inch clear floor space, and a toilet compartment, with a wheelchair user shown at scale. Label each dimension.

Source/materials: Bobrick Planning Guide, pp. 9-13 and 17-18

LevelDescription
BeginningDrawings not to scale or dimensions missing.
DevelopingMostly to scale; several dimensions wrong or unlabeled.
