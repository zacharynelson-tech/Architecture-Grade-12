Cycle 8 - Construction Documents II - Architectural & MEP

The architectural set and its engineering partners: how everything is drawn, scheduled, and coordinated.

12.7.412.7.512.9

Learning Objectives

Understand

Identify the types of architectural and MEP drawings and the information each contains.

Apply

Read and interpret architectural and MEP drawings, including symbols, schedules, and notation.

Create

Create architectural drawings to industry standards: floor plans, elevations, building and wall sections, details, interior elevations, and schedules.

Lesson Outline

▸
  • Architectural Drawings
    Floor plans, elevations, building sections, wall sections, details, interior elevations, and schedules.
  • Reading Schedules
    Schedules and their components in architectural drawings.
  • MEP Drawings
    Mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and fire protection plans; reflected ceiling plans.
  • MEP Symbols & Sequencing
    Symbols and notation in mechanical and electrical drawings; when MEP drawings are developed in the design timeline.
  • Revit Studio Day
    Produce an architectural sheet set to industry standards.
  • Revit Studio Day (cont.)
    Add interior elevations and schedules; check coordination.
  • Wrap-Up
    Review for the cycle.

Independent Assignments

Reading + Guided Notes

Schedules, Drawing Conventions, and Building Systems
▸

Read the drawing-conventions and schedules guidance, then the mechanical and electrical systems sections. Complete the guided notes on how schedules and MEP drawings communicate information.

Source/materials: Architectural Graphic Standards, CAD Standards module on schedules and drafting conventions (PDF p. 534); Building Construction Illustrated, Chapter 11 Mechanical and Electrical Systems (PDF p. 373). MEP symbols and the sequencing of MEP drawings: source TBD.

LevelDescription
BeginningNotes largely incomplete; schedule and MEP drawings confused.
DevelopingMostly complete; limited explanation of how information is communicated.
ProficientComplete and accurate; explains what schedules and MEP drawings show and why.
ExemplaryAll Proficient criteria met, plus identifies a conflict between architectural and MEP information.

Reflection Check-In

Formative - complete after Reading & Guided Notes
▸

Formative: not scored on the rubric scale. A checkpoint before moving into hands-on work. Write a few sentences for each prompt.

  • Which do you think is harder to coordinate, architectural and structural drawings or architectural and MEP drawings? Why?
  • What is one thing a schedule can say more clearly than a drawing?

Software Exercise - Architectural Sheet Set

Build a standards-based architectural set
▸

Produce architectural sheets for a provided or prior model: floor plan, elevations, building section, wall section, one interior elevation, and a door and window schedule. Plot them with title blocks.

Source/materials: Your prior model or a provided model

LevelDescription
BeginningSeveral required sheets missing; standards not followed.
DevelopingAll sheets present with errors in line weights, annotation, or schedules.
ProficientAll required sheets present, accurate, and consistent with industry drawing standards.
ExemplaryAll Proficient criteria met, plus clean coordination across sheets.

Spatial Organization

Plan organization and stacking
▸

Diagram how the spaces in a multi-story commercial plan are organized (public vs. service, circulation, and stacked restrooms). Annotate how the organization supports MEP efficiency.

Source/materials: Provided plan or your own plan

LevelDescription
BeginningDiagram unclear; organization not explained.
