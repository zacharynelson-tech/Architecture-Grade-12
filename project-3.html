Project 3 - Mass Timber

Your third studio project: a mass timber building, with a brief you write and get approved.

12.9Semester 2Student-written brief

Deliverables

Every studio project has the same eight deliverables, so you can see your growth from one building to the next.

  • Project brief and program. One page: building type, occupancy group, users, rooms and approximate areas, and the site.
  • Code summary sheet. Occupancy group, construction type, height and area check, means of egress (exit access, exits, exit discharge), fire protection notes, and accessibility items.
  • Site and zoning check. Setbacks, buildable area, lot coverage, parking including accessible spaces, and a short site analysis (sun, wind, topography, water).
  • Revit plan set. Floor plans, two elevations, and one building section, annotated to drawing standards.
  • Wall section. A detailed wall section that shows your structural system and the control layers (water, air, thermal, vapor).
  • Accessible restroom. One restroom layout that meets ADA clearances, modeled in Revit and placed on a sheet.
  • Energy check. Total R-value and U-value of one wall and one roof assembly, compared with the energy code requirement for your climate zone.
  • System rationale. One page explaining why this structural system suits your building and what it cost you in design decisions.

MASS TIMBER

Semester 2 Design Studio · Project 3 of 3

The Challenge

Write your own project brief. Choose a client, a building type, a program, and a site, and propose them to your teacher in the proposal below. Once it is approved, design your building around a mass timber structure and show that it meets code, zoning, and accessibility requirements.

Constraints
  • Occupancy group: Business (B) or Educational (E).
  • Construction type: Type IV is the heavy timber type in BCI p. 54. Mass timber code provisions: teacher to confirm against the building code edition your district enforces.
  • Size: Your teacher sets the number of stories and the total floor area for this project.
  • Zoning: Your building must fit the setbacks, height, and lot coverage of its site.
  • Accessibility: Your design must meet the ADA, including one accessible restroom.

What mass timber asks of you

  • Expose or conceal the structure, and say why. Heavy timber is often left exposed, so joint and connection detailing matters (BCI pp. 142 to 143).
  • Check member sizes against the minimum sizes the code requires for heavy timber (BCI p. 142, p. 54).
  • Plan for MEP. Timber floors often have no concealed space for piping, wiring, and ductwork, so decide where services run (BCI p. 142).
  • Address sound. Plank-and-beam floors are prone to impact sound transmission (BCI p. 142).
  • Show how fire is handled, including the noncombustible exterior walls Type IV requires (BCI p. 54).

Reading to use: BCI 4.35 Wood Beams (p. 139), 4.38 to 4.39 Plank-and-Beam Framing (pp. 142 to 143), 2.06 Types of Construction (p. 54). Cross-laminated and dowel-laminated timber: source TBD.

Your Proposal

Before you start designing, submit a one-page proposal. Your teacher must approve it. It should include:

  1. The client and the building type
  2. Occupancy group and a reason for choosing it
  3. Program: rooms and approximate areas
  4. The site, with its zoning district and a reason it suits the building
  5. Why mass timber suits this building
  6. Size: number of stories and approximate total floor area

Checkpoints

Your teacher sets the dates for each checkpoint.

  1. Brief. Program and site are settled. For Project 3, your proposal is approved before you continue.
  2. Code and site. Code summary and site and zoning check are done and checked against the rubric.
  3. Schematic plans. Plans, elevations, and a section are modeled in Revit.
  4. Structure and envelope. Structural system is laid out and the wall section shows its layers.
  5. Accessibility and energy. Restroom layout and the energy check are complete.
  6. Final sheets. Sheets are plotted, the rationale is written, and you have scored yourself against the rubric.
