Cycle 4 - Site Analysis & Design

Before a building exists, the site has already made decisions about it.

12.4.112.4.212.4.312.4.412.3.212.3.312.9

Learning Objectives

Understand

Identify common zoning requirements, the steps of a site analysis, and the survey types used in construction.

Apply

Complete a zoning analysis for a site and analyze the existing site to locate a building with the least impact and the most passive-design benefit.

Create

Create a site analysis, a cut-and-fill exercise, and a parking and site layout; build a Revit massing study and set up a central model.

Big Ideas & Essential Questions

▸
12.4 - Site Analysis and Design for Commercial Construction
Big Idea(s)
  • Site analysis is an assessment of a potential construction site to determine its suitability for the intended development.
Essential Question(s)
  • How do zoning regulations affect site development?
  • What goes into a successful site analysis and design?
12.3 - Building Information Modeling for Commercial Architecture
Big Idea(s)
  • BIM promotes collaboration between all stakeholders of a project to improve project efficiency and delivery.
  • BIM enhances the capabilities of architects by providing them with powerful tools for design exploration, collaboration, data analysis, and project management.
Essential Question(s)
  • How can BIM be beneficial over traditional CAD?
  • What are the best practices for creating a comprehensive and accurate BIM model?

Lesson Outline

▸
  • Zoning Regulations
    Purpose of zoning, districts, allowed uses, and bulk requirements (setbacks, height, coverage). Locating a town's zoning regulations.
  • Zoning Analysis
    Use a town's regulations to complete a zoning analysis giving setbacks, maximum height, and maximum coverage for a site.
  • Site Analysis
    Solar orientation, wind, flora and fauna, topography, and water/wetlands. Steps of a site analysis.
  • Site Design
    Topography alteration: cut and fill and grading. Hardscapes (parking lots, walkways, pervious vs. impervious). Native vs. invasive vegetation. Stormwater management.
  • Surveying
    Angles, areas, and distance; elevations. Boundary, Existing, As-built, and A2 surveys.
  • Revit Studio Day
    Conceptual massing and massing in place on a sloped site.
  • Revit Studio Day (cont.)
    Type properties, model sharing, and central model setup.
  • Wrap-Up
    Finalize site analysis and layout. Review for the cycle.

This is a rough outline and is subject to change.

Reading + Guided Notes

Site Analysis, Zoning, and Site Design
▸

Read the chapter sections on topography, regulatory factors and zoning ordinances, circulation, and parking. Complete the guided notes: what to analyze on a site, how zoning limits a building, and how grading and cut and fill reshape a site.

Source/materials: Building Construction Illustrated, Chapter 1 The Building Site: Section 1.07 Site Analysis; Section 1.10 Topography (PDF p. 21, including cut and fill); 1.24 Regulatory Factors (p. 34); 1.25 Zoning Ordinances (p. 35); 1.26 Site Access and Circulation (p. 36); 1.29 Vehicular Parking. Surveying and survey types (Boundary, Existing, As-built, A2): source TBD. Zoning regulations of a local town: student-located.

Formative - complete after Reading & Guided Notes
