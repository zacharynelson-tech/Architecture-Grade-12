Cycle 2 - Building Codes II - Construction Types, Fire Protection & Egress

How a building is built, how it protects people from fire, and how everyone gets out.

12.1.412.1.512.1.612.3.112.9

Learning Objectives

Understand

Differentiate Types I through V construction, active vs. passive fire protection, and the three components of the means of egress.

Apply

Analyze a project to determine its type of construction, and identify when a fire wall is required over a fire partition.

Create

Create an egress and fire-protection diagram for a commercial floor plan, and extend your Revit schematic set with openings, stairs, sections, and annotation.

Big Ideas & Essential Questions

▸
12.1 - Building Codes
Big Idea(s)
  • It is essential for an Architect to be able to read and understand how building codes are applied.
  • Early integration of building codes into the design process benefits the design.
Essential Question(s)
  • What is the purpose of building codes?
  • Why are there different building codes required for different building types?
  • How do you read and apply building codes for your design?
12.3 - Building Information Modeling for Commercial Architecture
Big Idea(s)
  • BIM promotes collaboration between all stakeholders of a project to improve project efficiency and delivery.
  • BIM enhances the capabilities of architects by providing them with powerful tools for design exploration, collaboration, data analysis, and project management.
Essential Question(s)
  • How can BIM be beneficial over traditional CAD?
  • What are the best practices for creating a comprehensive and accurate BIM model?

Lesson Outline

▸
  • Types of Construction
    Type I through Type V: what each is, how they differ, and how to analyze a project to determine its type. Where heavy timber and mass timber fit.
  • Fire & Smoke Protection
    Active vs. passive systems. Fire wall, fire barrier, fire partition, smoke barrier vs. smoke partition: characteristics of each and when a fire wall is required over a fire partition.
  • Means of Egress
    The three components: exit access, exit, exit discharge. The purpose of each and the egress requirements that apply to them.
  • Applying It to a Floor Plan
    Diagram egress paths and fire separations on a provided commercial plan.
  • Revit Studio Day
    Fenestration (doors and windows) and section cutting.
  • Revit Studio Day (cont.)
    Annotation: text, dimensions, labels, and symbols.
  • Wrap-Up
    Finish your egress diagram and Revit set. Review for the cycle.

This is a rough outline and is subject to change.

▸

Source/materials: Building Construction Illustrated, Section 2.06 Types of Construction (PDF p. 54), Section 2.07 (fire walls and occupancy separations, PDF p. 55), and Section 9.05 Stair Requirements (PDF p. 317). Mass timber / Type IV and the IBC egress chapter: source TBD.
